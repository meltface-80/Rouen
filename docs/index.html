Rouen + HQPWeb

A music-discovery companion and full-screen wall display for Roon — random-album browsing, a shelf of covers to flick through, instant whole-library search, rich label & artist pages, and seamless playback with Roon Server at the heart. A Rouen is a duck — MusicD is short for Music Duck. Called MusicD Remote until v1.8.74; install commands, the image and the data volume keep their musicd-remote names.

+ HQPWeb. For a Roon that plays through HQPlayer, Rouen has hqpweb built in — statelycurmudgeon's web controller for HQPlayer, and their hard work, carried here with thanks under its MIT licence. What it does and how to use it →

Features

Everything talks to your existing Roon Core through the official extension API — the app never changes your library files. An optional read-only /music mount adds file-tag labels, release dates, local waveforms, the local-files badge and the format filters. Tap i on any feature for how to set it up and use it.

📚ShelfNew

Flick through your collection the way you once flicked through a rack of CDs or records — made for a tablet on a stand or a TV. A shelf of covers turns under your finger: a short swipe moves one album, swipe and hold keeps it turning, and a hard flick spins it to land on an album three seconds later. Tap the front cover and the case turns over to show its tracks. Three looks — Covers on a glossy shelf, Spines in a CD rack, and a Carousel. Choose what is on it by Genre, by Artist letter (A–Z, # and 1–9) or Random; with nothing chosen it is the whole library, A → Z by artist. Play now, Play next, Add to queue under the front cover, and it opens on the record that is playing.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to switch on. Open ☰ → Shelf, tap Shelf › on the wall display, or point a browser at http://<server-ip>:3399/shelf. Tap a genre or letter to choose it and again to undo; Clear all × clears a section. Pick a look with the three buttons above the shelf (each device remembers its own); Spin does what a flick does, and a mouse wheel, a trackpad and the arrow keys turn it too. Tap the zone at the bottom to play somewhere else. ‹ Remote (top left) goes back to the app, and Wall Display › (top right) shows while the wall display is switched on.

📺Remote ⇄ wall displayNew

☰ → Wall display turns the remote into the wall display for its zone, and ‹ Remote in its top-left corner brings it back exactly as you left it — always on screen now, faint, and one tap: off-white over a dark screen, grey over a light artist photo. Shelf › sits opposite it, top right. A screensaver timer switches to the display after 1 to 60 minutes untouched — per device and off unless you choose it. It waits while Settings, a sheet or a selection is open.

How to set it up and use it. First switch on Settings → Wall Display → Wall display; the menu entry and the screensaver only appear while it is on. For the screensaver, on the device you want it on, pick a time under Settings → Wall Display → This device → Switch to the wall display. On the display, tap ‹ Remote to go back, or anywhere else to reveal the mode buttons.

🎚️HQPlayer control, from hqpweb

statelycurmudgeon's hqpweb, built in: a screen in the side menu for HQPlayer's 1x and Nx filters, its modulator or dither and presets, with Signalyst's ratings. Every change is read back; one that stops playback or that HQPlayer can't keep up with is put back by itself and remembered. A guide (beta) says where to start for your DAC, and Find your DAC looks it up. Several HQPlayers — found on your network or added by address — and several DACs behind one HQPlayer, each with its own answers, learned failures and presets.

How to set it up and use it. Switch on ☰ → Settings → HQPlayer → HQPlayer control, then Find HQPlayers and Add yours (or add it by address; the control port is 4321). Open ☰ → HQPlayer. The full steps are in Rouen + HQPWeb below.

💾Backup & restore

Back up your settings, playlists and Dynamic Playlists, Listen later, and API keys and sign-ins — any of the four. Backups are kept on the server (your last 10, plus 5 taken before a restore) and each can be downloaded. A restore keeps a copy of how things were first, restarts the server, and the page reloads by itself. A downloaded backup can be restored from the file.

How to set it up and use it. Open ☰ → Settings → Backup & restore, switch on the parts you want and tap Back up now. Each backup has Restore, Download and Delete; Restore from a file… takes one you downloaded. On an installed iPhone or iPad app, Download opens the share sheet — choose Save to Files. A backup with your API keys holds your sign-ins: keep it somewhere private.

⏭️Play next, everywhere

Play now, Play next, Queue on every track in the album view, and Play next in the selection menu for selected tracks and albums and in the album's ⋯ menu. What you choose plays straight after the current track — selected tracks in album order, selected albums in the order you picked them.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up. Tap a track for its buttons, or long-press tracks or albums to select several (the one you pressed is already picked) and choose Play next.

📱Album view & mini player

On tablets and desktops About this album sits under the cover, beside the tracks, and on a tablet the album view fills the screen. The mini player is taller with bigger buttons on touch screens, and on a desktop it is a card in the corner that can be dragged anywhere. Now playing shows the whole cover, framed, and the seek bar is a brass level meter where a track has no waveform.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up. On a desktop, drag the mini player by anything but a button; double-click it to send it back to its corner. Choose a theme under Settings → Setup → UI Settings → Theme.

🎛️UI Settings & label search

Make the app fit the screen it is on: text size for album and artist names and for each grid screen's title, a grid layout of Auto, 3 columns, 2 columns or List, and a tile size from −50% to +50% for album and label tiles everywhere. The Labels screen gains a search and a #–Z / Z–# order button, Home Screen rows drag smoothly into place, and a Smart Pick opens its album in your library or on your default streaming service.

How to set it up and use it. Open ☰ → Settings → Setup → UI Settings and pick from each list; it applies at once and is saved on that device only. On the Labels screen, tap the magnifying glass to search (× clears, then closes) and #–Z to reverse the order.

🐳A ready-made image

Install with one docker run — nothing to download or build — from ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote, built for x86 and 64-bit ARM: PCs and NAS boxes, a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 on a 64-bit OS, Apple Silicon Macs. Updates stay one tap in the app, and switching an existing install needs no reinstall: remove the old container and run the image with the same volume, and your Roon pairing and history carry over.

How to set it up and use it. Build your command with the configurator below and run it. In Roon, go to Settings → Extensions and click Enable on Rouen, then open http://<server-ip>:3399 on any device. The image and the data volume keep their musicd-remote names, so an existing install carries on unchanged.

🔄Your library, followed in seconds

Add albums in Roon and they are in the app within about a minute, with no rescan. While the app or the wall display is open it checks Roon every 30 seconds (every 3 minutes when nobody is looking) with three tiny calls that cost the Core almost nothing, follows a change until Roon settles, then reads the library once — so a big import costs one pass, not one per album. The side menu says when the library was last checked.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to switch on. To force a fresh read, open the side menu (☰) and tap the Rescan library line at the bottom — it also shows the album count and when Roon was last checked.

🧭Simpler navigation

The ☰ menu button is Home's; on every other screen the brass ‹ takes its place — back to the album or screen you came from on an artist page (or a label opened from an album), Home anywhere else. A shorter side menu — Pitchfork, Labels, Qobuz, Tidal, Listen later, Discover, Dynamic Playlists, Playlists, Wall display, Shelf, HQPlayer, then Rescan library and Settings — with Random albums and Smart Picks opening from their Home rows and Import at the top of the Playlists screen. Settings is grouped as Services, Playback, Wall Display, HQPlayer, Setup and Updates (with Backup & restore since v1.8.84); Setup holds Smart Picks, Record labels, Home Screen, UI Settings, Share Card, Discover and API Keys. On a phone every page opens full screen with its title pinned; on a tablet or desktop the list is a side panel.

How to set it up and use it. Open the side menu (☰, top left on Home) and choose Settings at the bottom. Tap a row to open its page; the back arrow returns one level and × closes Settings. A setting with an ⓘ explains itself when you tap it. Switch the Random albums or Smart Picks row off under Settings → Setup → Home Screen and that screen comes back to the side menu.

🕒Listen later

Put an album aside to play another time — from its ⋯ menu, from a selection of albums on any wall, or with ＋ Listen later on a Smart Pick. Roon's own Listen later can't be reached from an extension, so this is the app's own list: kept on the server, the same on every device, and safe across a library rescan. Albums you own play straight from it; a Smart Pick that isn't in your library yet can be added in one tap or opened in Qobuz or TIDAL. It tidies itself: an album comes off once every track has been played through since you put it aside, on any zone, from any app.

How to set it up and use it. Choose Listen later from an album's ⋯ menu, or long-press albums on a wall to select several. Find the list under ☰ → Listen later or on its Home row (switch the row on or off and move it under Settings → Setup → Home Screen). To send each day's Smart Picks here, set Settings → Setup → Smart Picks → Send each day's picks to → Listen later.

↔️Previous & next album

Swipe the album card sideways, tap the chevrons on the cover's edges, or use the arrow keys to step to the albums either side — on the screen you opened it from, whether that's a Home row, the Library wall, or an artist or label page. Close the card and you land on the album you stepped to.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to set up. Open an album from any row or wall, then swipe, tap ‹ › on the cover's edges, or press ← / →. An album opened from search or Now playing has no neighbours.

📡Live screens

Every screen keeps itself up to date. Home, the Library and Not played walls, the Queue, the album, artist and label pages, Smart Picks and Discover refresh themselves when something changes — a play, a release date found, a setting changed on another device. In place: your scroll position stays, nothing flashes “Loading…”, and nothing moves while your finger is on it.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to switch on. Every open device checks the server every few seconds while it is on screen — so an album played on one phone leaves Album of the day on every other device too.

🧭Discover

New records by the artists you actually listen to. Read from your play history rather than your library, and ranked by how many separate days you played an act rather than by play count. Albums only, from the last two months, never a record you already have — and a remaster is refused only when the title says so and the act has an older release under the same name. Every row goes somewhere: in your Roon library it queues, otherwise it opens in your default streaming service. Uses Deezer's public catalogue and never touches your Roon Core.

How to set it up and use it. Switch on Settings → Setup → Discover → Discover (off by default) and pick the hour under Look for new records at (server time). It looks once a day; Refresh on the same page looks now. Then open ☰ → Discover. It works from your play history, so it gets better the more you listen. Your default streaming service is set under Settings → Setup → Share Card → Default.

📤Share card

Tap Share on any album or on Now playing and the record gets a card of its own — artwork, artist, title, release date and label, a description, and the Pitchfork score with its Best New Music flag where there is one. Underneath: where to hear it (Qobuz, TIDAL, Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, Deezer, Bandcamp — and Qobuz opens the Qobuz app) and where to read about it (Wikipedia, Pitchfork and AllMusic for the album; Wikipedia and AllMusic for the artist if you want them). And “If you like this” suggests three acts worth hearing next, weighed by what you play — two you haven't heard near the acts you play most, and one you know with a record you don't own yet, each saying why. One in your Roon library is queued; one that isn't opens in your default service.

How to set it up and use it. Tap the share button in an album or on Now playing. Choose which services and review links appear, and the default service, under Settings → Setup → Share Card; holding a service button under the card also sets the default for that device.

〰️Waveform

The seek bar on Now playing and the wall display's progress strip draw the shape of the track you're listening to — where the quiet intro ends, where the loud middle is. Every bar is the level of its slice of the track, measured from both channels at full rate, and the shape sits under the playhead at every point in the track. Each track is analysed once and stored, and the next one in the queue is prepared while the current one plays. Works for local files and for Qobuz and TIDAL streams — Roon sends those straight to your endpoint and never to an extension, so the track is fetched from the service with your own account, turned into 4,000 loudness values and discarded. The audio you are listening to is never touched, no audio is written to disk, and Roon still handles all playback. Anything the app can't identify keeps the plain bar.

How to set it up and use it. Switch on Settings → Playback → Waveform (off by default). Local files need your music mounted at /music; Qobuz and TIDAL tracks need that service connected under Settings → Services. Play something and open Now playing — the first play of a track takes a second or two to analyse.

Rouen + HQPWeb

For a Roon that plays through HQPlayer, Rouen has hqpweb built in — the web controller for HQPlayer by statelycurmudgeon. Rouen's HQPlayer screen is their work: how HQPlayer is talked to, every change checked and undone when it stops playback, the filter guide (the ratings in it are Signalyst's own), the modulator and dither guide and its sources, Find your DAC, presets, and finding and keeping several HQPlayers. Rouen carries it with thanks, under hqpweb's MIT licence, in Rouen's own look.

hqpweb's repository: github.com/statelycurmudgeon/hqpweb — its own README, changelog and releases, and the place to report anything about the HQPlayer side. Neither hqpweb nor Rouen plays music: both talk to HQPlayer through its published control protocol (TCP port 4321), and Roon sends HQPlayer the music.

Using it in Rouen

You need HQPlayer with control from the network allowed, on a computer the Rouen server can reach on TCP 4321. hqpweb has been tested with HQPlayer Desktop 5 and HQPlayer 6 Embedded; Desktop 6 and Windows are untested.

  1. Switch it on. ☰ → Settings → HQPlayer → HQPlayer control. It is off by default, and while it is off nothing connects to anything.
  2. Add your HQPlayer. Under Your HQPlayers, tap Find HQPlayers and Add the one you want — or type its address under Add by address (port 4321 unless you changed it), tap Test, then Add. Find uses multicast, so it sees HQPlayers on the Rouen server's own network, and only when Rouen runs with --network host (the standard install does). On macOS with Docker Desktop, on Unraid's br0, or for an HQPlayer on another VLAN, add it by address.
  3. Open ☰ → HQPlayer. Tap 1x filter, Nx filter or Modulator (Dither in PCM) to choose; a ✓ marks what HQPlayer reports is running. A choice HQPlayer's rules say won't play, or one that failed here before, is marked before you tap it. Undo puts back the last change; Presets saves and applies whole sets.
  4. Use the guide. Answer Your setup in Settings → HQPlayer, or the questions on the Modulator sheet's Guide tab; Find your DAC is under the PCM question in Settings → HQPlayer → Your setup.
  5. More than one HQPlayer? Add each one and switch with the HQPlayer picker at the top of the HQPlayer screen.
  6. More than one DAC behind one HQPlayer? Under Settings → HQPlayer → DACs, name each one with Add a DAC, and whenever you switch DACs in HQPlayer itself, choose the same one with the DAC picker — HQPlayer can't tell another app which DAC it is using. The guide's answers, the settings learned not to work and a DAC's own presets follow your choice.
Left to Roon and Rouen, on purpose: the volume (Rouen's own volume control is the one to use; a preset never raises HQPlayer's volume more than 6 dB at once, the automatic undo never raises it, and Undo returns to a louder level only if nobody has moved it since), play, pause, skip and seek, and setting the output rate and mode by hand (the guide's rate-and-modulator pairs and its Switch to PCM are the ways in). HQPlayer's profiles and output device can't be switched from Rouen or hqpweb — its control protocol doesn't allow it; use HQPlayer's own Client or HQPlayer Embedded's web page. No login: anyone who can reach Rouen or hqpweb can change HQPlayer, as anyone who can reach port 4321 already can — keep them on a network you trust, never on the internet.

Using hqpweb on its own

hqpweb also runs by itself, without Rouen, as a page of its own on port 4380 — everything above (named DACs arrive in its next release), plus HQPlayer's own volume, the output rate and mode, convolution, matrix profile, polarity, the 20 kHz filter, adaptive volume, seeking in files HQPlayer plays itself, and an optional link to Roon. It is in beta. These steps are from hqpweb's README (0.1.0-beta.3), which is always the current version.

  1. Docker on a machine that can reach HQPlayer on TCP 4321 (amd64 or arm64 — a Raspberry Pi 4/5, an ARM NAS, Apple Silicon).
  2. Run it with Docker Compose — save the first box as docker-compose.yml in a new folder and run docker compose up -d there — or with the docker run in the second (Synology, Unraid, Portainer).
  3. Open http://<that machine's IP>:4380 and go to Settings → General → Add to add your HQPlayer's address (leave the name blank to use HQPlayer's own). On a phone, Add to Home Screen makes it a full-screen app.
  4. Roon (optional): in hqpweb's Settings → Roon, switch it on and Find the Core (or enter its address, port 9330); in Roon → Settings → Extensions, enable hqpweb; then back in Settings → Roon, pick the Roon zone that feeds each HQPlayer. Its container must reach the Core on TCP 9330, and each install of hqpweb needs its own approval in Roon.
name: hqpweb
services:
  controller:
    image: ghcr.io/statelycurmudgeon/hqpweb:latest
    container_name: hqpweb
    restart: unless-stopped
    init: true
    ports:
      - "4380:4380"
    volumes:
      - config:/config # your instances, presets and settings
volumes:
  config:
docker run -d --name hqpweb --restart unless-stopped --init \
  -p 4380:4380 -v hqpweb_config:/config \
  ghcr.io/statelycurmudgeon/hqpweb:latest
Updating: docker compose pull && docker compose up -d — or docker pull ghcr.io/statelycurmudgeon/hqpweb:latest, then docker rm -f hqpweb and the same docker run; the hqpweb_config volume keeps your settings. Skim hqpweb's changelog first. Finding HQPlayers ("Scan now") uses multicast, so it needs host networking (Linux only) — hqpweb's README shows the override file that turns it on. Its other options (the image version to stay on, port, interface, host names behind a reverse proxy) are there too. Rouen uses port 3399 and hqpweb 4380, so the two don't collide on one machine.

From then on, updates are one tap in the app.

Unraid

Roon never shows Rouen under Settings → Extensions? On Unraid the usual cause is the network, not the app.

Why. On Unraid, Roon Server normally runs on the br0 custom network (macvlan/ipvlan) with an IP address of its own, which is what RAAT needs. A container on --network host sits on Unraid's own interface instead, and Unraid's kernel keeps the two apart: the discovery broadcast never reaches the Core, and by default the host cannot even open a connection to a br0 address. The fix is to put Rouen on br0 too, with its own free IP, and tell it where the Core is with ROON_CORE_IP, which connects straight to the Core instead of discovering it.

  1. Pick two addresses. ROON_CORE_IP is your Roon Core container's br0 IP (Roon → Settings → General shows it). Rouen needs a different one: a free address on your LAN, outside your router's DHCP range. The examples use 192.168.1.50 for Roon and 192.168.1.60 for Rouen.
  2. Run it with Compose (the Docker Compose Manager plugin) or docker run, below. Change the music path to your share and name: br0 to your custom network's name if it differs.
  3. Open the app at Rouen's own address, http://192.168.1.60:3399 (not the Unraid server's), then click Enable in Roon → Settings → Extensions.
services:
  musicd-remote:
    image: ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote:latest
    pull_policy: always
    container_name: musicd-remote
    restart: unless-stopped
    networks:
      br0_net:
        ipv4_address: 192.168.1.60   # a free address for Rouen
    environment:
      TZ: "Europe/London"
      ROON_CORE_IP: "192.168.1.50"   # your Roon Core's br0 address
    volumes:
      - /mnt/user/appdata/musicd-remote:/app/data
      - /mnt/user/data/media/music:/music:ro

networks:
  br0_net:
    external: true
    name: br0   # your Unraid custom network
docker run -d \
  --name musicd-remote \
  --pull always \
  --restart unless-stopped \
  --network br0 \
  --ip 192.168.1.60 \
  -e TZ=Europe/London \
  -e ROON_CORE_IP=192.168.1.50 \
  -v /mnt/user/appdata/musicd-remote:/app/data \
  -v /mnt/user/data/media/music:/music:ro \
  ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote:latest
Check it reached the Core. Once Rouen is enabled in Roon, docker logs musicd-remote shows [roon] paired with core …. A line [roon] cannot reach Roon Core at 192.168.1.50:9330 — retrying every 10s means the address is wrong or blocked; it keeps retrying, so nothing needs restarting once it is fixed. The image has no ping; this tests the Core's port from inside the container:
docker exec musicd-remote node -e "require('net').connect(9330,'192.168.1.50').on('connect',()=>{console.log('reachable');process.exit()}).on('error',e=>{console.log(e.message);process.exit(1)})"
Rather keep host networking? Set ROON_CORE_IP anyway, and let the host reach br0: Settings → Docker, stop the Docker service, set Host access to custom networks to Enabled, start it again. The app is then on the Unraid server's own address.
Your data. These examples keep Rouen's data in /mnt/user/appdata/musicd-remote, Unraid's usual place. If you already ran it with the musicd-remote-data volume, use -v musicd-remote-data:/app/data instead (or copy the volume's contents into the folder first), or Roon will ask you to authorize Rouen again and your history starts empty.

API keys

All free, all optional — but they significantly improve artwork and label coverage. Paste each one into the app under ☰ → Settings → Setup → API Keys, then Save — the box shows a ✓ once the service has accepted the key. Label logos also need Settings → Setup → Record labels switched on.

Free

FanArt.tv

Record-label logos for labels that have a MusicBrainz MBID, and the wall display's artist photos.

  1. Register (or sign in) at fanart.tv/get-an-api-key and request a personal API key
  2. Follow the on-screen prompts — the key is shown on that page once you're logged in
  3. Copy the key
  4. In the app: ☰ → Settings → Setup → API Keys → paste into FanArt.tv key → Save (a ✓ shows it works)
Free

Discogs

Finds label names that iTunes and MusicBrainz miss, and fetches label logos.

  1. Sign in (or register) at discogs.com
  2. Settings → Developers → Generate new token
  3. Copy the token
  4. In the app: ☰ → Settings → Setup → API Keys → paste into Discogs token → Save (a ✓ shows it works)

Environment variables

Everything the container understands — the configurator above sets the ones you need.

VariableDefaultPurpose
PORT3399HTTP port for this app
ROON_CORE_IP(discover)Roon Core address for setups where multicast discovery can't reach it — macOS / Docker Desktop, or a Core on its own network such as Unraid's br0 (see Unraid). The extension then connects straight to the Core
ROON_CORE_PORT9330Roon Core API port used with ROON_CORE_IP — only change it if your Core runs its API on a non-standard port
MUSIC_DIR/musicWhere the optional read-only music mount lives inside the container — the scan reads everything under it, so several folders can be mounted as subdirectories. Used for file-tag labels, release dates, local waveforms, the local-files badge and the format filters
TZEtc/UTCThe container's local time. Sets when Album of the day turns over (00:01) and the hour Smart Picks and Discover run. The 6- and 12-month "not played" windows are counted in absolute time and are unaffected
RRA_DISCOGS_KEY(unset)Seeds the Discogs token on a fresh data volume, so label logos work from the first scan. A token saved in Settings wins over it, and an env-seeded one is not written to disk — unset the variable and the key is gone
RRA_FANART_KEY(unset)Seeds the FanArt.tv key the same way
RRA_DEBUGon in DockerVerbose logging (timestamps, Roon API call traces with durations). On by default inside Docker — 0 for quiet logs

First run

The container is up — four steps and you're listening.

  1. Enable the extension in Roon. Roon → Settings → Extensions — Rouen (publisher MusicD) appears there once the container is running. Click Enable.
  2. Open the app. Browse to http://<your-server-ip>:3399 from any phone, tablet or desktop on your network. Add it to your home screen for an app-like experience.
  3. Add your API keys. ☰ → Settings → Setup → API Keys → paste in the keys from above. For labels, switch on Settings → Setup → Record labels and let the label scan run.
  4. Optional: wall display and Shelf. Switch on Settings → Wall Display, then point a TV or spare tablet at http://<your-server-ip>:3399/display — or at /shelf to flick through your covers.
