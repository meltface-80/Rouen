Rouen + HQPWeb

A music-discovery companion and full-screen wall display for Roon — random-album browsing, a shelf of covers to flick through, instant whole-library search, rich label & artist pages, and seamless playback with Roon Server at the heart. A Rouen is a duck — MusicD is short for Music Duck. Called MusicD Remote until v1.8.74; install commands, the image and the data volume keep their musicd-remote names.

+ HQPWeb. For a Roon that plays through HQPlayer, Rouen has hqpweb built in — statelycurmudgeon's web controller for HQPlayer, and their hard work, carried here with thanks under its MIT licence. What it does and how to use it →

Features

Everything talks to your existing Roon Core through the official extension API — or, with the optional native mode, as Roon's own apps do — and the app never changes your library files. An optional read-only /music mount adds file-tag labels, release dates, local waveforms, the local-files badge and the format filters. Tap i on any feature for how to set it up and use it.

📱Rouen for AndroidNew

New in v2.0: the Rouen app makes your Android phone a Roon zone, at home and away from home. All the music still comes from Roon. Rouen carries its sound to the phone as Opus 256 or lossless, through Tailscale built into Rouen when you are out. There's no second server and no extra streaming login. Also: downloads for offline listening, and Android Auto.

How to set it up and use it. See Rouen for Android below: setting it up, using it, what it doesn't do, and why the music might drop out.

🪟Native on Windows

Since v1.9.67, Rouen runs on a Windows PC as one file — no Docker, no Node.js to install. Download Rouen-v2.0.2-windows-x64.exe from the release, run it, and it unpacks itself, opens Rouen in your browser and runs in its own window. It updates at the same time as every other install, by the same one tap, and your pairing, account and history are kept through every update.

How to set it up and use it. Rouen runs in a black window on your computer and you use it in your web browser. See Install on Windows below for simple step-by-step instructions.

🎛️Native mode

Rouen talks to your Roon Core the way Roon's own apps do, both ways. Your library is the Core's own album list. A heart in Rouen is Roon's favourite. Listen later is one list shared with Roon. Dynamic Playlists are Roon's smart playlists, with their covers, and playlists made in Rouen are Roon's own. Add to library uses Roon's own search, and play, pause, skip, seek, volume, mute, shuffle, repeat and Roon Radio go straight to the Core. New in v1.9.66: each zone's crossfade, volume levelling and sleep timer, its DSP in a window of its own (each item on or off; Headroom, Crossfeed and Sample rate conversion's settings; Parametric EQ bands), and Edit for any of Roon's smart playlists, in Rouen's Focus editor. A status board in Settings → Native mode shows a green, red or grey light for each, and why when one fails; the official extension API is the fallback for everything.

How to set it up and use it. Native mode is chosen once, at install or at the first start after updating to v1.9.65, and can then be switched on and off under Settings → Native mode. Read the notice there first: native mode is unofficial, not made or supported by Roon Labs, and uses the private protocol Roon's own apps use — a Roon update can break it until Rouen is updated. Keep port 9332 on your own network. Rouen's own playlists, Listen later and Dynamic Playlists move into Roon by themselves; the status board says what moved and what couldn't, and why. Zone settings and DSP are under the bar's zone button ("Play on"), or the Shelf's zone menu: tap DSP for its window. A smart playlist is edited from its page under Dynamic Playlists; one the editor can't show whole, or an edit Roon can't hold, is refused with the reason.

🔭More around every album

Below the review in every album: More by the artist and the records they appear on, from your library, then Similar artists and Similar albums from Last.fm — three of each, with More for the rest. Last.fm is read-only: a free key, no sign-in, nothing scrobbled. Also new: Random Album asks before it plays (Play now, Play next or Queue), Random album radio doesn't play an album again within six months, and Smart Picks start on Nowhere — ask me rather than going straight into your library. Fixed in v1.9.2: with a genre filter on, an album opened from an artist's page no longer answers "Album not found".

How to set it up and use it. Open any album and scroll below the review. More by and Appears on need nothing; the Similar rows need a free Last.fm key under Settings → Setup → API Keys. A similar artist or album in your library opens in the app, the rest are marked Last.fm ↗ and open on Last.fm. Choose where Smart Picks go under Settings → Setup → Smart Picks → Send each day's picks to.

📚Shelf

Flick through your collection the way you once flicked through a rack of CDs or records — made for a tablet on a stand or a TV. A rack of spines, as tall as the screen, turns under your finger: a short swipe moves one album, swipe and hold keeps it turning, and a hard flick spins it to land on an album three seconds later. Each spine reads, from the top, the artist, the album and its version (Deluxe Edition, a remaster), and from the foot up its catalogue number, year and format (FLAC 24/96, MP3, Qobuz, TIDAL). Choose what is on it by Genre, Artist letter, Year, Label or Type, and file it in any Order; with nothing chosen it is the whole library, A → Z by artist, and it opens on the record that is playing. Along the bottom is a slim bar: play/pause, what is playing, where you are on the shelf, Spin, the zone and its volume.

The queue is a pane on the right — tap a track to play from there — and small tabs at the edges fold the choices away to the left and the queue to the right; with both away the shelf has the whole screen.

New in v1.9.68: tap the album in front and it turns into a full-screen album view: Play now and Queue under the cover, the album's review, more by the artist (tap one to go to it) and every track — hold a track to choose it, tap more to add them, and play just those. On a phone held upright the album in front is a tall card with its details under the cover. The catalogue numbers and kinds come from your files' tags, then Discogs, then — with native mode on — your Roon server.

New in v1.9.69: on a phone the choices open full screen from the tab at the top of the shelf, and Show N albums at their foot takes you back to the shelf; a tablet or a computer keeps the side pull-out. A phone's spines carry the catalogue number at their foot too.

New in v1.9.70: two more tabs: Formats, your albums by format and resolution ("24/96", "DSD64 2CH", "MP3"), and Favourites, the albums hearted in Roon. Genres can be sorted by count or A → Z, and with native mode on they come from your Roon server.

New in v1.9.71: on a desktop, a TV or a tablet on its side, the album you open from the Shelf is the library's three columns — the cover, its buttons and the credits at the left, About this album in the middle, the tracks with their lengths at the right.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to switch on. Open ☰ → Shelf, tap Shelf › on the wall display, or point a browser at http://<server-ip>:3399/shelf. The first time, a popup shows the gestures; tick Don't show again to stop it reappearing after each update. Tap a genre, letter, decade, label or type to choose it and again to undo; once two are chosen in a section, Clear all × clears it. Spin (in the bar) does what a flick does, and a mouse wheel, a trackpad and — once the shelf has been clicked — the arrow keys turn it too. The bar's zone button plays somewhere else, and its speaker opens the volume. Tap the album in front for its album view; Back, × or Escape closes it. The fold-away tabs sit at mid-height on each side (on a tablet held upright the choices fold up, from a tab below them, and the queue is a drawer over the shelf; on a phone the choices cover the screen and Show N albums closes them); each device remembers which sides it left folded, and the queue reads nothing from Roon while it is folded. ‹ Remote (top left) goes back to the app, and Wall Display › (top right) shows while the wall display is switched on — and then, on a device with a screensaver time, Shelf goes to the wall display after that long untouched.

🎚️HQPlayer control, from hqpweb

statelycurmudgeon's hqpweb, built in: a screen in the side menu for HQPlayer's 1x and Nx filters, its modulator or dither and presets, with Signalyst's ratings. 💾Backup & restore

Back up your settings, playlists and Dynamic Playlists, Listen later, and API keys and sign-ins — any of the four. Backups are kept on the server (your last 10, plus 5 taken before a restore) and each can be downloaded. A restore keeps a copy of how things were first, restarts the server, and the page reloads by itself. A downloaded backup can be restored from the file.

How to set it up and use it. Open ☰ → Settings → Backup & restore, switch on the parts you want and tap Back up now. Each backup has Restore, Download and Delete; Restore from a file… takes one you downloaded. On an installed iPhone or iPad app, Download opens the share sheet — choose Save to Files. A backup with your API keys holds your sign-ins: keep it somewhere private.

🐳A ready-made image

🔄Your library, followed in seconds

Add albums in Roon and they are in the app within about a minute, with no rescan. While the app or the wall display is open it checks Roon every 30 seconds (every 3 minutes when nobody is looking) with three tiny calls that cost the Core almost nothing, follows a change until Roon settles, then reads the library once — so a big import costs one pass, not one per album. The side menu says when the library was last checked.

How to set it up and use it. Nothing to switch on. To force a fresh read, open the side menu (☰) and tap the Rescan library line at the bottom — it also shows the album count and when Roon was last checked.

〰️Waveform

The seek bar on Now playing and the wall display's progress strip draw the shape of the track you're listening to — where the quiet intro ends, where the loud middle is. Every bar is the level of its slice of the track, measured from both channels at full rate, and the shape sits under the playhead at every point in the track. Each track is analysed once and stored, and the next one in the queue is prepared while the current one plays. Works for local files and for Qobuz and TIDAL streams — Roon sends those straight to your endpoint and never to an extension, so the track is fetched from the service with your own account, turned into 4,000 loudness values and discarded. The audio you are listening to is never touched, no audio is written to disk, and Roon still handles all playback. Anything the app can't identify keeps the plain bar.

How to set it up and use it. Switch on Settings → Audio Devices → Waveform (off by default). Local files need your music mounted at /music; Qobuz and TIDAL tracks need that service signed in there, under Qobuz waveforms or TIDAL waveforms (used for waveforms only). Play something and open Now playing — the first play of a track takes a second or two to analyse.

Rouen + HQPWeb

For a Roon that plays through HQPlayer, Rouen has hqpweb built in — the web controller for HQPlayer by statelycurmudgeon. Rouen's HQPlayer screen is their work: how HQPlayer is talked to, every change checked and undone when it stops playback, the filter guide (the ratings in it are Signalyst's own), the modulator and dither guide and its sources, Find your DAC, presets, and finding and keeping several HQPlayers. Rouen carries it with thanks, under hqpweb's MIT licence, in Rouen's own look.

hqpweb's repository: github.com/statelycurmudgeon/hqpweb — its own README, changelog and releases, and the place to report anything about the HQPlayer side. Neither hqpweb nor Rouen plays music: both talk to HQPlayer through its published control protocol (TCP port 4321), and Roon sends HQPlayer the music.

Using it in Rouen

You need HQPlayer with control from the network allowed, on a computer the Rouen server can reach on TCP 4321. hqpweb has been tested with HQPlayer Desktop 5 and HQPlayer 6 Embedded; Desktop 6 and Windows are untested.

  1. Switch it on. ☰ → Settings → HQPlayer → HQPlayer control. It is off by default, and while it is off nothing connects to anything.
  2. Add your HQPlayer. Under Your HQPlayers, tap Find HQPlayers and Add the one you want — or type its address under Add by address (port 4321 unless you changed it), tap Test, then Add. Find uses multicast, so it sees HQPlayers on the Rouen server's own network, and only when Rouen runs with --network host (the standard install does). On macOS with Docker Desktop, on Unraid's br0, or for an HQPlayer on another VLAN, add it by address.
  3. Open ☰ → HQPlayer. Tap 1x filter, Nx filter or Modulator (Dither in PCM) to choose; a ✓ marks what HQPlayer reports is running. A choice HQPlayer's rules say won't play, or one that failed here before, is marked before you tap it. Undo puts back the last change; Presets saves and applies whole sets.
  4. Use the guide. Answer Your setup in Settings → HQPlayer, or the questions on the Modulator sheet's Guide tab; Find your DAC is under the PCM question in Settings → HQPlayer → Your setup.
  5. More than one HQPlayer? Add each one and switch with the HQPlayer picker at the top of the HQPlayer screen.
  6. More than one DAC behind one HQPlayer? Under Settings → HQPlayer → DACs, name each one with Add a DAC, and whenever you switch DACs in HQPlayer itself, choose the same one with the DAC picker — HQPlayer can't tell another app which DAC it is using. The guide's answers, the settings learned not to work and a DAC's own presets follow your choice.
Left to Roon and Rouen, on purpose: the volume (Rouen's own volume control is the one to use; a preset never raises HQPlayer's volume more than 6 dB at once, the automatic undo never raises it, and Undo returns to a louder level only if nobody has moved it since), play, pause, skip and seek, and setting the output rate and mode by hand (the guide's rate-and-modulator pairs and its Switch to PCM are the ways in). HQPlayer's profiles and output device can't be switched from Rouen or hqpweb — its control protocol doesn't allow it; use HQPlayer's own Client or HQPlayer Embedded's web page. No login: anyone who can reach Rouen or hqpweb can change HQPlayer, as anyone who can reach port 4321 already can — keep them on a network you trust, never on the internet.

Using hqpweb on its own

hqpweb also runs by itself, without Rouen, as a page of its own on port 4380 — everything above (named DACs arrive in its next release), plus HQPlayer's own volume, the output rate and mode, convolution, matrix profile, polarity, the 20 kHz filter, adaptive volume, seeking in files HQPlayer plays itself, and an optional link to Roon. It is in beta. These steps are from hqpweb's README (0.1.0-beta.3), which is always the current version.

  1. Docker on a machine that can reach HQPlayer on TCP 4321 (amd64 or arm64 — a Raspberry Pi 4/5, an ARM NAS, Apple Silicon).
  2. Run it with Docker Compose — save the first box as docker-compose.yml in a new folder and run docker compose up -d there — or with the docker run in the second (Synology, Unraid, Portainer).
  3. Open http://<that machine's IP>:4380 and go to Settings → General → Add to add your HQPlayer's address (leave the name blank to use HQPlayer's own). On a phone, Add to Home Screen makes it a full-screen app.
  4. Roon (optional): in hqpweb's Settings → Roon, switch it on and Find the Core (or enter its address, port 9330); in Roon → Settings → Extensions, enable hqpweb; then back in Settings → Roon, pick the Roon zone that feeds each HQPlayer. Install on Windows

Rouen runs on your Windows computer as one file — nothing else to install. You then use Rouen in your web browser, on the computer or on a phone or tablet.

1. Start Rouen

  1. Download Rouen-v2.0.2-windows-x64.exe (the number is the version: take the newest) from the release page (under Assets). Keep the file — don't delete it after you run it.
  2. Double-click the file. If a blue box says "Windows protected your PC", click More info, then Run anyway.
  3. If a box asks about the network, click Allow.
  4. A black window opens with some text in it. Leave it open — that window is Rouen. Closing it stops Rouen.
  5. After a few seconds, Rouen opens in your web browser. If it doesn't, open your browser and type localhost:3399 in the address bar, then press Enter.
  6. In Roon, go to Settings → Extensions and click Enable next to Rouen.

2. Use it on your phone or tablet

  1. On the computer, find its address: Windows 11: Start → Settings → Network & internet → Properties. Windows 10: Start → Settings → Network & Internet → Properties. Look for IPv4 address — something like 192.168.1.20.
  2. On the phone or tablet (on the same Wi-Fi), open the browser and type that address followed by :3399 — for example 192.168.1.20:3399.
  3. Add /display to the end for the wall display, or /shelf for the Shelf.

3. Start Rouen automatically (optional)

  1. Right-click the Rouen file you downloaded and choose Create shortcut (on Windows 11, choose Show more options first). A new file appears ending in "- Shortcut".
  2. Press the Windows key and R together. In the small box, type shell:startup and click OK. A folder opens.
  3. Drag the shortcut into that folder. Rouen now starts every time you turn on the computer.
Updates. Rouen tells you when a new version is out: tap Update and it updates itself, the same as every other install.

(For the curious: they are in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Rouen\app\data, a hidden folder; type that into File Explorer's address bar to open it. Music files. The optional /music features (file-tag labels, catalogue numbers, local waveforms) read your music files; on Windows they work when Rouen runs on the same computer as your music library.

Rouen for Android — your phone as a Roon zone

New in v2.0. The Rouen app turns your Android phone into a Roon zone, at home and away from home. All the music still comes from Roon: your library, and TIDAL and Qobuz through Roon's own sign-ins. There is no second music server to run and no extra streaming-service login. Rouen carries Roon's sound to your phone, through Tailscale built into Rouen when you are out.

What it does

  1. Your phone appears in Roon as a zone named Rouen followed by the phone's name, for example Rouen Pixel 8. Play to it from Rouen or from Roon's own apps. Roon's queue, Radio, play counts, DSP and volume levelling all work as on any zone.
  2. Rouen's whole page is in the app: Home, Library, the Shelf, Settings, everything. The notification and lock screen show what Roon is playing, and their buttons control Roon.
  3. At home and away, the same way. On your home Wi-Fi the app reaches Rouen directly. On mobile data or another Wi-Fi it reaches Rouen through Tailscale, built into both Rouen and the app. Away from home, only your phone's own zone is shown and it is selected for you; your other zones come back when you are home.
  4. Two qualities, chosen separately for home and away: Opus 256 kbps (the default; small, and sounds excellent) or Lossless (FLAC at the original resolution).
  5. Downloads: save albums from your own music files to the phone, and play them anywhere, even with no connection at all.
  6. Android Auto: Recently played, Smart Picks, Random albums, Library A–Z, Playlists and Downloaded albums on your car's screen.

How the music gets to your phone

The sound takes this path, and every step stays inside things you already run:

  1. Roon Server plays to your phone's zone, exactly as it plays to any Squeezebox. The audio leaves Roon lossless.
  2. Rouen, on your own machine next to Roon, receives it on the phone's player. It turns it into one continuous stream for the phone: Opus 256, or lossless FLAC.
  3. That stream goes to the app: over your home Wi-Fi, or over the internet through Tailscale when you are away. Tailscale is a private, encrypted link between Rouen and your phone; nothing is opened to the internet.
  4. The app keeps a buffer of music ahead and plays it to the phone's speaker, your headphones or your car.

This keeps everything coming through Roon, with nothing to host beyond Rouen itself and no streaming service to sign in to again.

Set it up

You need: Rouen v2.0.2 or newer, running and paired with Roon; an Android phone (Android 8 or later, 64-bit); and, for use away from home, a free Tailscale account.

  1. In Roon, go to Settings → Setup and turn Squeezebox support on.
    If you also run Lyrion Music Server (formerly Logitech Media Server) on the same network, stop it while you use Rouen for Android. Lyrion takes priority over Roon for Squeezebox and Squeezelite players, and with it running, Rouen away from home won't work.
  2. On the phone, open the release page and download rouen-android.apk from the newest release (under Assets). Open it and install it. If Android asks, allow your browser to install apps.
  3. Open Rouen on the phone, at home on your Wi-Fi. It finds Rouen by itself; if it doesn't, type Rouen's address with :3399, for example 192.168.1.20:3399. Give the phone a name, and sign in to Rouen as you do in the browser.
  4. In Roon, the zone Rouen + your phone's name appears. In its Device Setup, set Volume Control to Fixed volume, and use the phone's own volume buttons.
    Don't rename this zone in Roon. Leave it as Roon names it, Rouen + your phone's name. The app finds its own zone by that name: renamed, it is lost, and away from home you see no zones at all. For a different name, give it on the app's first screen, where it asks for the phone's name.
  5. For away from home: in Rouen, go to Settings → Setup → Away from home. Tap Sign in for Rouen's server, then Sign in under This phone. Use the same Tailscale account for both. Until you sign in, Rouen's built-in Tailscale stays off and contacts no Tailscale server, and it never sends Tailscale's diagnostic logs.
  6. Choose the quality: go to Settings → Audio Devices, tap your phone, and scroll to Streaming quality at the bottom. Set On home Wi-Fi and Away to Opus 256 kbps or Lossless (FLAC). A change is used from the next time Roon starts playing to the phone.

Use it

  1. To play on the phone, choose your phone's zone in the zone picker and play anything, as on any zone. Pause, skip and the volume work from the app, the lock screen and the notification.
  2. Leaving home: nothing to do. When the phone leaves your Wi-Fi the other zones drop off, your phone's zone is selected, and the music carries on through Tailscale.
  3. Downloads: open an album's ⋯ menu and choose Download to this phone. Downloaded albums have their own row on Home, and play with no connection. Quality, folder, size limit and Wi-Fi only are under Settings → Setup → Downloads & Offline.
  4. Android Auto: plug the phone into the car, or connect wirelessly, and choose Rouen. Because the app is installed from a file rather than the Play Store, Android Auto only lists it once you turn on Unknown sources: in the Android Auto settings, tap Version ten times, then ⋮ → Developer settings → Unknown sources.
  5. Updates: the app tells you when there is a newer one, and updates itself with one tap.
  6. Something not right? Turn on Settings → Setup → Logging, use the app until it happens, then tap Save log and send the file with your report. It holds no personal data.

What it doesn't do

  1. No iPhone or iPad app. On those, Rouen's page in Safari works as before, as a remote.
  2. It needs Rouen and Roon Server running, and your home internet up, to stream. With either off, the app plays only your downloads.
  3. Not bit-perfect. Lossless arrives unaltered, but the phone plays it through Android's own mixer at the phone's volume.
  4. No downloads of TIDAL or Qobuz albums, ever. Only albums from your own files can be downloaded.
  5. No search in Android Auto yet.
  6. Not alongside Lyrion Music Server. With Lyrion running on the same network, it takes priority for Squeezebox players, and Rouen away from home won't work. Stop Lyrion while you use the app.
  7. The phone's zone isn't always in Roon. About 15 minutes after you last used the app with nothing playing, the app rests and its zone leaves Roon. Open the app and it is back.

Why the music might drop out

The music travels from your home to wherever you are, so it can only be as steady as the connection along the way. The app keeps a buffer ahead to ride over short gaps; music stops only when that buffer runs out. What can cause that:

  1. Weak or no mobile signal: tunnels, trains, lifts, the countryside. Playback carries on from the buffer, then pauses until the signal comes back.
  2. Switching between Wi-Fi and mobile data. The connection moves to the new network; the buffer usually covers it, but a slow change can leave a short gap.
  3. Your home's upload speed. Away, every note leaves your home through your internet's upload. Opus 256 needs about 0.3 Mbit/s (about 115 MB of mobile data an hour); lossless needs about 1 Mbit/s for CD quality (about 400 MB an hour) and 3–6 Mbit/s for high resolution. Lossless away is the likelier to drop out.
  4. Tailscale relaying. When your networks don't allow a direct link, Tailscale passes the stream through one of its relays, which is slower.
  5. The phone's battery saver can pause apps in the background. Set Rouen's battery use to Unrestricted (Settings → Apps → Rouen → Battery).
  6. A pause, skip or seek takes a moment, because the phone has to follow Roon over the network.

For the steadiest listening away, keep Away on Opus 256, or download what you'll want where the signal is poor.

Unraid

Roon never shows Rouen under Settings → Extensions? On Unraid the usual cause is the network, not the app.

Why. On Unraid, Roon Server normally runs on the br0 custom network (macvlan/ipvlan) with an IP address of its own, which is what RAAT needs. A container on --network host sits on Unraid's own interface instead, and Unraid's kernel keeps the two apart: the discovery broadcast never reaches the Core, and by default the host cannot even open a connection to a br0 address. The fix is to put Rouen on br0 too, with its own free IP, and tell it where the Core is with ROON_CORE_IP, which connects straight to the Core instead of discovering it.

  1. Pick two addresses. ROON_CORE_IP is your Roon Core container's br0 IP (Roon → Settings → General shows it). Rouen needs a different one: a free address on your LAN, outside your router's DHCP range. The examples use 192.168.1.50 for Roon and 192.168.1.60 for Rouen.
  2. Run it with Compose (the Docker Compose Manager plugin) or docker run, below. Change the music path to your share and name: br0 to your custom network's name if it differs.
  3. Open the app at Rouen's own address, http://192.168.1.60:3399 (not the Unraid server's), then click Enable in Roon → Settings → Extensions.
services:
  musicd-remote:
    image: ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote:latest
    pull_policy: always
    container_name: musicd-remote
    restart: unless-stopped
    networks:
      br0_net:
        ipv4_address: 192.168.1.60   # a free address for Rouen
    environment:
      TZ: "Europe/London"
      ROON_CORE_IP: "192.168.1.50"   # your Roon Core's br0 address
    volumes:
      - /mnt/user/appdata/musicd-remote:/app/data
      - /mnt/user/data/media/music:/music:ro

networks:
  br0_net:
    external: true
    name: br0   # your Unraid custom network
docker run -d \
  --name musicd-remote \
  --pull always \
  --restart unless-stopped \
  --network br0 \
  --ip 192.168.1.60 \
  -e TZ=Europe/London \
  -e ROON_CORE_IP=192.168.1.50 \
  -v /mnt/user/appdata/musicd-remote:/app/data \
  -v /mnt/user/data/media/music:/music:ro \
  ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote:latest
Check it reached the Core. Once Rouen is enabled in Roon, docker logs musicd-remote shows [roon] paired with core …. A line [roon] cannot reach Roon Core at 192.168.1.50:9330 — retrying every 10s means the address is wrong or blocked; it keeps retrying, so nothing needs restarting once it is fixed. The image has no ping; this tests the Core's port from inside the container:
docker exec musicd-remote node -e "require('net').connect(9330,'192.168.1.50').on('connect',()=>{console.log('reachable');process.exit()}).on('error',e=>{console.log(e.message);process.exit(1)})"
Rather keep host networking? Set ROON_CORE_IP anyway, and let the host reach br0: Settings → Docker, stop the Docker service, set Host access to custom networks to Enabled, start it again. The app is then on the Unraid server's own address.
Your data. These examples keep Rouen's data in /mnt/user/appdata/musicd-remote, Unraid's usual place. If you already ran it with the musicd-remote-data volume, use -v musicd-remote-data:/app/data instead (or copy the volume's contents into the folder first), or Roon will ask you to authorize Rouen again and your history starts empty.

Thanks to the Unraid user who worked this out and shared it.

All free, all optional — but they significantly improve artwork and label coverage, and Last.fm adds similar artists and albums to every album page. Record-label logos for labels that have a MusicBrainz MBID, and the wall display's artist photos.

  3. Copy the key
  4. In the app: ☰ → Settings → Setup → API Keys → paste into FanArt.tv key → Save (a ✓ shows it works)
Free

Discogs

Finds label names that iTunes and MusicBrainz miss, and fetches label logos.

  1. Sign in (or register) at discogs.com
  2. Settings → Developers → Generate new token
  3. Copy the token
  4. In the app: ☰ → Settings → Setup → API Keys → paste into Discogs token → Save (a ✓ shows it works)
Free

Last.fm

Similar artists and similar albums at the foot of every album page — opened in the app when they are in your library, on Last.fm when not. Read-only — nothing is scrobbled.

  1. Sign in at last.fm, or sign up free — any Last.fm account will do
  2. Open last.fm/api/account/create (the Get an API account link on last.fm/api)

Uninstall

Find how you installed Rouen below. Each way removes three things: the container (or service), the image, and the data — your Roon pairing, play history, settings, playlists, Listen later, API keys, the Qobuz and TIDAL sign-ins, backups and logs, all kept in one place. Your music is never touched: the /music mount is read-only.

Want to keep anything? Skip the step that deletes the data, and a later install pointed at the same volume or folder carries on where this one stopped. Or take a backup first: ☰ → Settings → Backup & restore → Back up now, then Download. It carries settings, playlists, Listen later and keys (not play history or the Roon pairing), and Restore from a file… brings it back. If your user isn't in the docker group, put sudo in front of each docker command.

Docker (docker run) — Linux, NAS, macOS, Windows

# 1. Stop and remove the container
docker rm -f musicd-remote

# 2. Delete the data volume (skip to keep it)
docker volume rm musicd-remote-data

# 3. Remove the image — list first: there may be more than one tag
docker images ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote
docker image rm ghcr.io/meltface-80/musicd-remote:latest
